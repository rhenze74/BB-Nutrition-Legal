Support

Answers to common questions. Something else? Email support@buzzerbeaterlabs.com — in the app, Settings › Contact Support fills in your app version for us.

01Logging

How do I log food?

On Today, tap Add Food under a meal. Search your own foods, the built-in database (over 13,000 foods from the USDA — everyday foods and dishes like pad thai, burrito bowls or tortilla soup — plus popular dishes like chicken satay, tikka masala or shawarma, estimated from a typical recipe) and — online — packaged products, pick an amount and tap Add. Foods you've logged before show up under Recent, so logging them again takes two taps.

Why can't I find a brand?

Brands like Premier Protein, Quest or Kirkland are under Products in Add Food search, found online in USDA Branded Foods (about 450,000 US products, from the makers' labels) and Open Food Facts (a free community database). Type the brand and the product (“premier protein shake”) and pause. Only what you type is sent — nothing about you — and Settings → Food Search turns it off. Still missing? Scan the barcode, or scan the nutrition label once; after that it's in your foods for good. Open Food Facts entries are added by volunteers, so check one against the label if a number looks off.

Can I log by describing my meal?

Yes — Add Food → Describe a Meal. Type it or tap Say It and talk: “two scrambled eggs, toast with butter and a coffee.” BB Nutrition finds each food and amount for you to check, then adds them all. Your voice is turned into text on your iPhone and never sent anywhere.

How does barcode scanning work?

Add Food → Scan Barcode. BB Nutrition checks your own foods first, then looks the number up on Open Food Facts, a free public database, and then USDA Branded Foods. Only the barcode number is sent. If a product isn't there, scan its nutrition label instead — next time you scan it, it'll be found instantly.

Can it read a nutrition label?

Add Food → Scan Nutrition Label. Take a photo of the Nutrition Facts panel; the numbers are read on your iPhone and shown for you to check before saving.

What's Quick Add for?

When you just know the calories (and maybe macros) — a restaurant meal, say. It logs the numbers without a food behind them.

Can I save a meal I eat often?

Yes. On Today, tap ⋯ next to the meal and choose Save as Meal, give it a name (“Post-workout shake”) and pick which foods to keep. Then it's under Meals in Add Food, in Foods → Meals, and in Siri: “Log Post-workout shake in BB Nutrition”. When you log it you can leave out a food or change the servings.

Can I plan meals ahead?

Yes. Go to tomorrow (or any day ahead) with the arrow and add foods as usual — they're planned: shown faded, and not counted in your totals or sent to Apple Health. On the day, tap the circle next to a food once you've eaten it, or tap ⋯ → Mark as Eaten for the whole meal. Anything you skip never counts. To plan later today, open a food and turn on Planned. Meal reminders list what's planned, with a Mark as Eaten button.

Is there a grocery list?

Yes. Foods → the cart button. Tap + to add recipes (pick how many servings of each), the meals you've planned for the coming days, or any item. The same ingredient from several recipes is added up (2 cups + 1 cup of rice = 3 cups) and everything is grouped by aisle. Tap an item to tick it off. ⋯ → Add to Reminders puts the list in a Groceries list in Apple's Reminders app, or Share List sends it as text. A recipe's page also has ⋯ → Add to Grocery List.

Can I repeat a meal?

Tap ⋯ on a meal to copy it to today or the next day. An empty meal offers “Same as Yesterday”, and a past day has Copy This Day to Today. You can also long-press any entry.

Can it remind me to log?

Settings → Meal Reminders. Pick breakfast, lunch and dinner times; you only get a reminder when nothing's logged for that meal yet. Tap it to add the meal, or press and hold it and tap Log Usual to log the same foods as last time without opening the app.

Can I log with Siri or the Home Screen?

Yes. Say “Log my usual breakfast in BB Nutrition” to log the same foods as the last time you had breakfast, “Log water in BB Nutrition” for a glass of water, or “How many calories do I have left in BB Nutrition”. You can log one of your foods by name (“Log oatmeal in BB Nutrition”) once you've logged it in the app. Find them all in the Shortcuts app, where you can also put one on the Action button. For the Home Screen, add the BB Nutrition widget: it shows calories left and your macros, and the larger size has a + to add food.

02Recipes

How do recipes work?

Foods → Recipes. Type the ingredients the way a recipe does (“2 cups rice”, “1 lb chicken breast”), set the servings, and BB Nutrition works out the nutrition per serving. Log a serving from the recipe, or find it in Add Food like any other food.

Can I import a recipe from a website, Instagram or a cookbook?

Foods → + → Import Recipe, then paste the link. Most recipe websites work. Instagram usually doesn't let apps read a post without signing in, so copy the caption instead (tap the caption → Copy) and paste that. For a cookbook page, a recipe card or a screenshot, tap Take a Photo or Choose Photos (up to 5 for a recipe that runs over pages) — the text is read on your iPhone. Check the matched ingredients — an orange ? means the amount was a guess.

Can I print my recipes as a book?

Foods → Recipes → the book button. Give it a title, pick the recipes and tap Make Book: you get a PDF with a cover, a contents page and each recipe on its own page, with the nutrition per serving. Share it to print at home, save it to Files, or send it to a print shop.

Can I share a recipe?

Open the recipe, tap ⋯ → Share Recipe. Send it as a picture — a card with the servings, calories and macros per serving, the ingredients and (if you like) the method — or as text.

03Goals & Progress

Where do my calorie targets come from?

At first, from your profile: the Mifflin–St Jeor formula for the calories you burn at rest, times your activity level, plus or minus your goal's weekly rate (3,500 calories per pound). Change them any time in Settings → Goals.

Can I eat more on training days?

Yes. Settings → your targets → Different Targets on Training Days, then set calories and macros for training days and for rest days. Training days come from your Buzzer Beater Fitness plan when it has days of the week (Fitness shares just the schedule), from any workout in Apple Health, or from the Training Day switch on Today — tap it to change any day yourself.

What is “Your Energy” on Progress?

Once you've logged about 10 full days and weighed in 4 or more times over 10+ days, BB Nutrition measures your real maintenance calories from your own data: what you ate, and how your weight moved. It suggests a new target — nothing changes until you tap to use it, and past days keep their old targets.

Why is my estimate “rough”?

Weight swings a few pounds day to day with water. More weigh-ins (ideally each morning) and fully logged days make the estimate steadier. Days you didn't finish logging are left out.

Is this medical advice?

No. Calorie estimates and targets come from standard formulas and your own data, and food values are approximate. If you have a medical condition, are pregnant, or are making a big change to your diet, talk to a doctor or registered dietitian.

Do I have to track vitamins and minerals?

No — they're off by default. Turn on Settings → Show Micronutrients to see them against the FDA's Daily Values. Foods that don't list a nutrient don't count toward it.

04Apple Health & Buzzer Beater Fitness

What does BB Nutrition share with Apple Health?

With Apple Health connected: everything you log (calories, macros and nutrients), water, weigh-ins, waist and body fat. It reads weigh-ins, waist and body fat from your scale and other apps, and active calories for context.

Does it work with Buzzer Beater Fitness?

Yes, through Apple Health: weigh-ins, waist and body fat logged in either app show up in the other, and your food is in Health for Fitness to use — its Progress → Nutrition shows your calories and macros next to your workouts and weekly training. Settings → Apple Health → Buzzer Beater Fitness opens it (or gets it from the App Store).

05Your Data

Where is my data kept?

On your iPhone. BB Nutrition has no accounts and no servers. The only things that ever leave your phone are what you choose to share with Apple Health, a barcode number when you scan, a recipe page you ask it to open, and Apple's App Store version check.

How do I back up my data?

BB Nutrition saves a backup every day (Settings → Backups & Data). Export one to iCloud Drive to keep it safe if you delete the app or get a new iPhone, then use Restore from a File there.

Read the privacy policy. In short: your data stays on your iPhone.
